# FPL Deadline and Team News Management

Reference document on running the weekly deadline process: where team news comes from, what to check before the deadline, how the Gameweek lock works, and a realistic light-touch routine. Pair this with the pre-season team-building document's GW1 final-week checklist for the season opener.

## Why the deadline process matters

- Every Gameweek ends at a hard lock: the deadline, typically 1–1.5 hours before the first kick-off of the Gameweek (GW1 2026/27 is Friday 21 August, 17:30 UTC). After it, transfers, captaincy and the bench order are frozen until the next Gameweek.
- The difference between a good and a bad deadline process is measured in points every week: a doubtful player left in the starting XI, a late injury not reacted to, or a bench order that blocks an auto-sub all cost points that no transfer can recover.
- Team news is the most volatile input in FPL — it arrives late, it changes fast, and it is the reason last-minute transfers exist.

## The weekly timeline

- **Press conferences** usually land Thursday–Friday before the deadline. Manager quotes on fitness, rotation and team selection are the primary team-news source.
- **Aggregators** collect it faster than any single club: Ben Dinnery's PremierInjuries on X/Twitter is the community standard for injury news; FPL Connect aggregates news and price changes.
- **Official FPL signals**: every player's game page carries a "news" field and a chance-of-playing percentage; a player tagged doubtful (`d`) in the official data is exactly the kind of flag to act on. The assistant's tools relay any DOUBTFUL note they see in the stats.
- **Line-up leaks** arrive from club reporters and aggregators through the deadline window — some clubs leak their XI hours early, others minutes before. Leaks are useful but never guaranteed; treat them as a tie-breaker, not a source.

## What to check before every deadline

- **Presser headlines for everyone in your starting XI and bench** — a doubtful tag on a starter changes the transfer plan; a doubtful on a bench player changes the bench order.
- **Late fitness tests** — players returning from injury are often declared fit at the presser or the eve of the game, not before.
- **Midweek cup and European rotation** — a team playing Tuesday/Wednesday is the classic weekend rotation risk; treat their attacking assets' minutes with caution even when the fixture looks easy.
- **International-break returns** — after international windows, late-arriving players are rested or subbed early; the first Gameweek back is the biggest rotation trap of the season.
- **The bench order** — auto-subs only replace starters who record zero minutes, and a one-minute cameo blocks the bench replacement. With four substitutes in set order, the order is a decision, not a formality.

## The light-touch routine

- The community's experience (the "20 minutes a day" thread) is that a serious season runs on far less time than most managers spend: a set-and-forget core plus Friday pressers and a deadline check is enough. The heavy hours — spreadsheets, content, tinkering — are optional, not required.
- A workable weekly minimum: (1) Friday — scan presser headlines for your 15, react to doubtfuls; (2) before the deadline — confirm the deadline time, set the bench order, pick the captain and vice-captain; (3) after the first kick-off — stop looking. Everything else is marginal gains.

## The discipline

- **Only confirmed transfers and sourced rumours count** — r/FantasyPL's rule is the right one: unsourced team-news gossip is noise.
- **Late news favours the template.** A highly-owned player is easier to swap at the deadline than a differential; if the news hits late, moving to the consensus pick costs less structure than reaching for a niche one.
- **Keep a plan B for every pick** so a deadline-day injury costs one transfer, not a wildcard — the same rule as the GW1 draft, applied all season.
- **Do not book transfers early in the week** when a presser or leak could change the plan; the free transfer is worth more on deadline day than on Monday.
