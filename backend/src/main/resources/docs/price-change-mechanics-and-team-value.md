# FPL Price Changes and Team Value

Reference guide to official price-change signals, transfer timing and the difference between market value and money available to spend. Rules and predictor guidance checked against official FPL sources on 18 October 2026. Examples are hypothetical, not live player forecasts. Pair this with FPL Transfer Strategy and FPL Deadline and Team News Management when choosing whether to act before team news.

## How and when prices change

For 2026/27, player prices can change at 00:00 UK time each day, after the season starts. Use Europe/London, not a fixed UTC offset: midnight is 23:00 UTC during British Summer Time and 00:00 UTC during winter. Demand in the transfer market drives changes, but FPL does not publish the full formula. Do not present a guessed net-transfer threshold as an official rule.

A daily rise or fall is £0.1m; FPL's introduction to the 2026/27 predictor states a maximum £0.3m move in a Gameweek. Transfer activity before the season starts does not cause these price changes. A rising price describes demand, not a player's expected points, fitness or suitability for your squad.

## Using the official Price Changes page

FPL introduced its official Price Changes page for 2026/27. It shows progress towards a price rise or drop, a projected progress figure after calibration, and classifications such as Likely to Rise or Very Likely to Drop. The published update interval is 15 minutes. The target price-change time is 00:00 UK time, using Europe/London for the calendar date and daylight-saving offset.

Progress is a measure of movement towards a threshold, not a probability. A projected 105% does not mean a 105% chance of rising. Separate the current progress from the projected progress at the target time; a player can be below the threshold now but expected to cross it before midnight. Use the official classification rather than inventing a confidence percentage.

An official rise/drop signal is useful for timing an otherwise justified transfer. Attribute it directly: "FPL's official forecast expects a rise at the next UK midnight." It is not a guarantee because transfer activity can change before the cutoff. A short qualification is enough; do not dismiss the official signal with a generic claim that price changes cannot be anticipated.

Use dated player evidence for a named forecast, including the read time and the target night. This reference does not supply a live player forecast. If no current tool evidence or dated user-provided official reading is available, say the player's forecast is unavailable rather than claiming to have checked the page. Calibration or missing data is not proof that the price will stay unchanged; respect an official price lock and its reported expiry rather than inventing a forecast. Do not carry an expired forecast forward to the following night or infer a forecast from historical price movement.

## Purchase price, market price and selling price

Purchase price is what you paid. Current market price is what it costs to buy the player now. Your personal selling price is what you receive when you sell. They are not interchangeable: on a rise you keep half the profit, rounded down to the nearest £0.1m; losses below the purchase price are taken in full.

| Purchase price | Current market price | Personal selling price |
|---|---|---|
| £7.0m | £7.0m | £7.0m |
| £7.0m | £7.1m | £7.0m |
| £7.0m | £7.2m | £7.1m |
| £7.0m | £7.3m | £7.1m |
| £7.0m | £7.5m | £7.2m |
| £7.0m | £6.6m | £6.6m |

A first £0.1m rise after buying at £7.0m therefore raises market value but not selling proceeds. Nor does every fall reduce proceeds: a player bought at £7.0m can fall from £7.3m to £7.2m while remaining sellable for £7.1m. Two managers holding the same player may have different selling prices. Check the purchase and selling prices on the manager's own transfer screen; public current prices do not reveal those personal values.

## Squad value, selling value and money in the bank

Market squad value is the sum of the squad's current market prices. Realisable squad value is the sum of personal selling prices. The bank is unspent cash. Do not treat a displayed squad-value total as cash available for transfers or assume it has already applied the profit deduction.

Hypothetical example: market squad value £104.0m, personal selling value £102.5m and bank £0.5m. Market value plus bank is £104.5m, but selling the entire squad would realise only £103.0m including the bank, before buying a legal replacement squad. For a single transfer, a £5.0m personal selling price plus £0.5m bank funds at most a £5.5m replacement, irrespective of the full squad's headline value.

A public price feed does not reveal your personal selling prices. If those are unknown, ask for them or give a conditional calculation; do not claim an exact available transfer budget. Rising market value can improve future options, but the gain is not all spendable and it is not all available without selling players you may want to keep.

## Deciding whether to transfer before a price change

First decide whether the incoming player improves the squad over the intended holding period. Then evaluate the official price signal, your actual bank and the outgoing player's personal selling price. Price movement can change when to make a good transfer; it does not by itself make a poor transfer good.

- **Act earlier when waiting can block a justified move.** With £5.0m selling proceeds and £1.0m bank, a £6.0m target is affordable now but not after a rise to £6.1m. Check team news, intervening matches and a credible alternative before choosing the financial benefit over the information lost by acting early.
- **Check both sides of the transfer.** Suppose the outgoing player was bought for £5.0m and is still worth £5.0m, the bank holds £1.0m and the target costs £6.0m. If the outgoing price falls to £4.9m and the target rises to £6.1m, the move becomes £0.2m short. Those are conditional expected changes, not confirmed events until observed. If a fall does not change your personal selling price because of profit rounding, do not count it as a loss of spendable funds.
- **Wait when headroom buys information.** If the plausible price changes still leave the move affordable, waiting for press conferences, recovery news or a midweek match may be more valuable. Keep an alternative at a workable price point.
- **Keep or roll when no football upgrade exists.** Do not sell a useful long-term player solely for a red price signal. Do not take a -4 hit just to gain or protect £0.1m: the transfer needs a points-based case over the holding period, not an invented conversion from money to points.

Official price forecasts concern money, not expected football returns. Recheck them close to the decision, state the evidence time, and keep injury/availability evidence separately attributed. A missing forecast should produce a conditional plan, not a guessed rise or fall.

## A practical price-monitoring routine

Track your intended purchases, likely sales and a small set of replacements rather than chasing every riser. Before a potential transfer, check their official signals, your bank and each outgoing player's personal selling price. Check again before the relevant UK midnight if price movement could block the plan; do not wait until the Gameweek deadline for a move that can become unaffordable overnight.

Keep a simple decision record: target, outgoing player, available funds, target night, the cost of waiting and the next important team-news update. If no funds are at risk, the normal deadline/team-news routine may be enough. A watchlist is a planning aid, not evidence that the assistant has a saved personal watchlist or an automatic price alert service.

## What captured daily price history can establish

The assistant's price-history tool reports captured daily prices, not per-gameweek closing prices. With consecutive successful daily captures, quote the old and new prices and their capture dates. A change between captures is an observed change over that interval, not a forecast. If captures are missing, report the wider interval: do not attribute a cumulative change to one particular night without dated tool evidence.

FPL's gameweek and season change counters provide broader totals, not the date of an overnight event. A prior forecast is not proof that a move happened. Conversely, a current forecast is not evidence about the price on a past night. Historical direction alone cannot establish another imminent rise or drop, nor does a past rise imply better future points.

If today's capture is unavailable, name the latest captured date rather than calling it today's price. Keep a current official signal, a captured historical price and a manager's personal selling price distinct in the answer, including when they come from different observation times.

## Team value through the season

Early in the season, preserving access to good players can improve later squad flexibility. Prefer gains that come with sensible long-term holds and affordable transfer routes; do not churn the squad merely to increase its displayed value. A £0.1m rise can be useful because a future buyer must pay more even when your own selling proceeds have not increased yet.

Near blanks, doubles or a wildcard, reassess both the points plan and the realisable budget. Late in the season, accumulated value is useful only if it helps buy players who can improve the remaining returns. Spending a saved free transfer or taking a hit just to protect headline value may work against that objective. See FPL Wildcard and Free Hit Strategy for permanent versus temporary chip effects and selling/rebuying costs.
