# FPL Chip Strategy

Reference document on the four FPL chips: function, optimal timing, Double Gameweek interplay, and season-long planning. Blank and double gameweeks themselves — when they happen, how far ahead they are known, and how to plan for them — are in `strategy-blank-and-double-gameweeks.md`.

## Chip overview

- **Two chip sets per season**: GW1–19 and GW20–38. Each set contains its own Wildcard, Free Hit, Bench Boost and Triple Captain — 8 chips total across the season.
- **Wildcard (×2, one per set)**: unlimited transfers for one Gameweek, no points cost. WC1 must be used by the GW19 deadline; WC2 is available GW20–38.
- **Free Hit (×2, one per set)**: unlimited transfers for one Gameweek; squad reverts afterwards. A temporary Wildcard.
- **Bench Boost (×2, one per set)**: all 15 squad players' points count for one Gameweek (normally only starting XI plus auto-subs).
- **Triple Captain (×2, one per set)**: captain points ×3 for one Gameweek instead of ×2.

**Key rules:**
- The first chip set expires at the GW19 deadline — any of its four chips left unused are lost; they do not carry into the second set.
- **One chip per Gameweek, with no exceptions.** A Wildcard cannot be played alongside a Bench Boost or a Triple Captain. The combination people mean is *sequential*: Wildcard one Gameweek to build the fifteen, Bench Boost the next.
- **There is no Assistant Manager chip in 2026/27.** The game carries four chip types, each in two sets; the manager chip trialled in an earlier season is not in this one.
- Chips can be activated/cancelled freely before the deadline; cannot be cancelled after it.
- Well-timed vs poorly-timed chips differ by 50+ points over a season.

## Wildcard strategy

The most flexible and valuable chip; used to correct mistakes, pivot to fixtures, or set up chip combinations.

**First Wildcard (GW1–19):**
- Optimal timing typically GW7–12: enough data to separate real performers from early-season flukes.
- Triggers: 3+ players need replacing; several teams' fixtures turn favourable simultaneously (~GW8–10); squad value collapse or restructuring needs; formation change.
- Avoid GW2–4 unless genuine crisis: early-season data is noisy.
- Must be played by the GW19 deadline — an unused WC1 is lost, not carried into the second set.

**Second Wildcard (GW20–38):**
- Often the strategic centrepiece: build a 15-player squad for a DGW Bench Boost, navigate blank Gameweeks, or restructure for the run-in.

## Bench Boost strategy

- Optimal: a DGW where all 15 players have two fixtures. Bench expected points roughly double (typical single-GW bench 8–12 pts → DGW bench 16–24).
- Preparation (weeks ahead): all 15 are guaranteed starters (a non-playing bench player wastes the slot); favourable fixtures across the squad; no blanks; budget spread across the bench rather than 4.0m enablers.
- Often paired with the Wildcard, in this order: the Wildcard builds the 15-strong squad one Gameweek, the Bench Boost is played the following Gameweek. They cannot share a Gameweek.
- Single-Gameweek Bench Boost is acceptable if the bench is genuinely strong and DGW options are poor (10–15 extra points is respectable).
- Common mistakes: using it too early; ignoring bench quality; timing it into heavy rotation periods (cups, European congestion, festive schedule).

## Triple Captain strategy

Highest single-Gameweek ceiling of any chip, but the most high-variance. A great TC earns 30+ extra points; a failed one earns roughly nothing extra.

- Gold standard: premium player, two easy home fixtures in a DGW (two fixtures roughly double expected return; tripling amplifies it).
- Strong single-Gameweek case: elite premium against the league's worst defence at home (e.g. Haaland vs a bottom side).
- See the dedicated captaincy/TC reference document for full criteria and failure modes.

## Free Hit strategy

- Primary use: blank Gameweeks (many teams without fixtures) or small DGWs where the squad cannot be adjusted without heavy hits.
- Also useful: loading up on one-week players (e.g. a DGW side) without committing transfers.
- Squad reverts after the Gameweek; the chip does not change long-term structure.

## Chip + DGW interplay

- DGWs cluster GW25–37 (largest GW34–37), caused by FA Cup, Carabao Cup and European rescheduling; blank Gameweeks precede them — entirely inside the second chip set (GW20–38).
- The first set's Bench Boost and Triple Captain (GW1–19) never overlap a DGW; save the DGW-chasing plan for the second set's BB/TC.
- Best combinations: Wildcard → DGW Bench Boost; Wildcard (or Free Hit) → navigate blanks then set up the DGW.
- Plan chip usage as a season-long sequence, not per-week decisions: identify likely blank/DGW windows early, keep chips flexible until schedules confirm (2–4 weeks before).

## Common chip mistakes

- Letting a first-set chip (GW1–19) expire unused at the GW19 deadline — it does not carry into the second set.
- Using any chip in a poor single Gameweek out of impatience.
- Bench Boost without a playable bench; TC without minutes certainty; Free Hit for one fixture.
- Over-committing to a chip plan before schedules are confirmed.
- Forgetting that only one chip can be active per Gameweek (Wildcard combos excepted).
