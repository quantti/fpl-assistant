# FPL Goalkeeper Strategy

Reference document on picking goalkeepers for 2026/27: how keepers score under the rebalanced bonus system, the price structure and viable pool, the premium-GK value question, £4.5m starters, rotation pairs, and set-and-forget versus rotation. Prices and ownership are the official FPL figures as of 16 August 2026 and move before the deadline.

## How goalkeepers score in 2026/27

- Clean sheets are worth 4 points; saves and bonus are the upside. Goalkeepers have a capped ceiling compared to outfield players — the question is always value, not ceiling.
- **The 2026/27 BPS changes are a genuine keeper buff.** The save points table was reworked: 3 BPS for a save from a shot inside the box, 2 BPS for any other save (crosses, deflections), 1 BPS for saving a "big chance", 8 BPS for a penalty save. A new "any save" category recognises sweeper-keepers who come out to claim crosses and corners — a small but real buff for keepers in dominant defences like Arsenal's, who face few shots but command their box.
- The BPS rebalance generally shifts bonus potential toward goalkeepers, full-backs, attacking midfielders and forwards, and away from DefCon-heavy centre-backs.

## The price structure and viable pool

The top-2k community guide (speardrops) frames the 2026/27 pool like this:

- **£6.0m — Raya** (Arsenal): the only keeper in his own tier.
- **£5.5m — Donnarumma** (Man City), **Alisson** (Liverpool): the premium-access keepers.
- **£5.0m — Lammens** (Man Utd), **Trafford** (Leeds), **Martínez** (Aston Villa): the mid-tier starters.
- **£4.5m — Verbruggen** (Brighton), **Kinsky** (Spurs), **Petrović** (Bournemouth), **Leno** (Fulham), **Rushworth** (Coventry), plus promoted starters Butland (Hull) and Scherpen (Ipswich).
- **£4.0m — Palmer** (Ipswich) and Dubravka (Spurs): bench enablers only.
- Rule out as first choices: Donnarumma and Alisson (too expensive for their save/bonus output), Trafford and Martínez (priced for teams that concede more than their price implies), and Rushworth (fine only inside a rotation, given Coventry's defensive outlook). A number of £5.0m keepers are backups or leaving (Kepa, Meslier at Arsenal; Jörgensen at Chelsea; Travers at Everton; Mamardashvili at Liverpool).

## The premium-GK value question

- Raya is the highest-scoring goalkeeper from last season (19 clean sheets, Arsenal's league-best underlying numbers) and the safest route into Arsenal's defence while Saliba is out. But the community verdict is mixed: his 0.87 non-penalty xGC per 90 is the best in the pool and his bonus output is joint-best, yet he faces only 1.6 saves per 90 — the lowest of the group — so his ceiling is capped.
- The structural argument against Raya: when you can buy a starting Arsenal defender at £5.5m (Mosquera, Calafiori), £6.0m on the keeper is hard to justify, and a £6.0m keeper is difficult to rotate out of.
- Donnarumma and Alisson are the classic "access" buys — secure starts at elite defences — but neither outscored cheaper keepers enough last season to justify £5.5m, and both squads have cheaper routes in.

## The £4.5m starter approach

- The 4.5 starter is this season's default value play, and **Verbruggen** is the community's best-in-class: Brighton conceded the third-fewest goals (46) with the fourth-fewest shots on target and fifth-lowest xGC, he posted a strong save rate on target, and the projection tools rate him the top-scoring keeper at the price over GW1–6 with early fixtures against Leeds and Coventry.
- **Kinsky** (Spurs) is the redemption story: Vicario (also £4.5m) is expected to leave, and Kinsky's late-season form after a nightmare start kept Spurs up — the arrival of De Zerbi improved the defence. **Petrović** (Bournemouth) and **Leno** (Fulham) are the other established 4.5 starters.
- The £4.5 starter approach pairs a 4.5 first choice with a £4.0m bench keeper, spending the savings in midfield and attack.

## Rotation pairs

- GK rotation is the alternative to set-and-forget: two starting keepers rotated by fixture — targeting promoted clubs, relegation candidates and home games, and timing rotation to maximise Bench Boost weeks.
- The community's favoured 2026/27 combos: **Lammens + Verbruggen** (popular pairing), with Lammens + Kinsky or Lammens + Petrović preferred by the top-2k guide — the logic being to pair a Man Utd keeper (good fixtures, Europe risk) with a 4.5 who faces weak attacks in the early weeks.
- Rotation works best when the two keepers' fixtures complement each other — when one faces a top side, the other faces a promoted club. The fixture ticker's clean-sheet percentages are the tool for this.
- The downside of rotation: both keepers can blank in the same week, and a benched keeper's save/bonus points are lost. Set-and-forget reduces the decision load and never benches a haul.

## Set-and-forget versus rotation

- Set-and-forget is the lower-variance choice: one keeper for the season (or until the wildcard), chosen for a strong defence and steady fixtures. Pickford (Everton £5.5m) is the classic profile — Everton top the early fixture ticker, and the community's Everton guide calls him a set-and-forget who finishes near the top of keeper scoring despite never being value.
- Pick one strategy and build around it: a rotation pair costs the same as a single premium keeper, and mixing the two wastes one bench slot.
- Bench Boost plans change the maths: a strong 15 with two playing keepers matters in a Bench Boost gameweek, which is when a rotation pair (or a 4.5 starter plus a playing 4.0/4.5) pays for itself.

## Bench keepers and enablers

- A £4.0m bench keeper is the cheapest slot in the game and the standard way to fund premiums. Dubravka (Spurs £4.0m, 22.3% owned) is the popular enabler — but high ownership is not role security: he is not the expected starter, so pair him with a starting first choice and never mistake popularity for minutes.
- Do not overspend on the keeper position: keeper points are capped, so the £0.5–1.5m saved by dropping from £5.5–6.0m to a £4.5m starter is better spent where ceilings are higher (midfield and attack).
