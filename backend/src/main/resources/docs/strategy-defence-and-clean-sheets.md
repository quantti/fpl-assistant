# FPL Defence and Clean-Sheet Strategy

Reference document on picking defenders for 2026/27: how defenders score now that defensive contribution (DefCon) points exist, clean-sheet-driven picks, the premium versus budget question, the budget bracket, and the "big at the back" structure debate. Prices and ownership are the official FPL figures as of 16 August 2026 and move before the deadline.

## How defenders score in 2026/27

- Appearance points rise from 1 for 1–59 minutes to 2 at **60+ minutes** (excluding stoppage time). A clean sheet adds 4 only after reaching 60 without conceding while on the pitch; a later goal after substitution does not remove it. These are FPL scoring facts. The tools rank full-fixture expected points without observed-minute, expected-minute or realised-points adjustments. Fresh available status and complete same-season, current-club evidence remain required; a sub-60 result in the latest five complete observations is a neutral factual note only, never an exclusion or ranking penalty. See `strategy-minutes-and-rotation.md`. Defenders lose 1 point for every two goals conceded while on the pitch; after a red card, subsequent team concessions still count.
- **DefCon points** (introduced in 2025/26) reward defensive contribution: clearances, blocks and interceptions. They are a separate, more predictable income stream than clean sheets or attacking returns — a centre-back who heads it, kicks it and blocks it banks DefCon points in most matches regardless of the scoreline.
- **The BPS was rebalanced for 2026/27** to reduce overlap with DefCon: clearances/blocks/interceptions now earn 1 bonus point per three (was per two), so DefCon magnets find bonus harder to come by; players no longer lose 1 BPS for being tackled (a buff to dribblers); and goalkeepers, full-backs, attacking midfielders and forwards gained bonus potential. Bonus points appear live from the 20th minute.
- The 2025/26 season proved the shift: seven defenders finished in the top-20 scorers, five of them centre-backs. Gabriel led all defenders (209 points), Guéhi placed in the top ten, Lacroix reached 154 points as the eighth-best defender almost entirely on the back of 40 DefCon points, and Van Hecke banked 30 DefCon bonuses.

## The DefCon meta

- DefCon output is **system-driven more than personnel-driven**: the community's evidence is that a direct, aggressive defensive style (Bournemouth under Iraola last season) generates far more clearances, blocks and interceptions than a possession-dominant one. When a manager who produced high DefCon numbers changes clubs, the question is whether the system travels with them.
- Pricing has absorbed the change: the classic £4.5m budget defender "fell off" last season, and DefCon-capable centre-backs are now priced at £5.0m and up. That is why this season's value bracket sits at £5.0m (new signings: Vuskovic, Ballard, Canvot, Botman, plus Van Hecke, Van de Ven, N.Williams) rather than at £4.5m.
- DefCon alone does not clear the bar: a player averaging ~10+ defensive actions per match earns roughly 2 bonus-equivalent points plus the 2-point appearance floor, which lands around 3–4 points per game — below the set-and-forget threshold. The community's refinement (hoya4life3381's EV analysis) is that the elite DefCon picks also carry goal threat or set-piece duty: Ampadu (3.6 ppg) and Andersen (3.7 ppg) stayed under the bar with no goals; Ballard, Keane and the corner-taking mids (Garner, Elliot Anderson) clear it. Look for **DefCon plus goal threat or corners**.

## Clean-sheet-driven picks

- Arsenal had the league's best defensive numbers last season (19 clean sheets, best underlying metrics) but are weakened at GW1: Saliba is out for an extended period (back) and Timber has a groin issue. Gabriel (£8.0m, 209 points last season) is the premium route; Mosquera (£5.5m) is the natural Saliba stand-in and the community's value pick into that backline.
- The elite defensive tier otherwise: Man City (Guéhi £6.0m — clean-sheet upside, rotation question under Maresca; O'Reilly £6.5m — attacking but rotation risk; Gvardiol £5.5m), Liverpool (Virgil £6.5m), Spurs under De Zerbi (Porro £5.5m, Van de Ven £5.0m, Van Hecke £5.0m, Senesi £6.0m).
- Fixtures drive clean-sheet picks early: Everton top the FFScout fixture ticker for the first six Gameweeks (Palace, Bournemouth, Ipswich and Hull among them), which makes Tarkowski (£6.0m), Branthwaite (£5.5m) and Pickford (£5.5m) the routes into their defence.

## Premium versus budget defenders

- The premium question is opportunity cost: Gabriel at £8.0m funds a £6.0m midfielder elsewhere. With Saliba out, a £5.5m Arsenal defender (Mosquera, Calafiori) or Raya at £6.0m delivers much of Gabriel's clean-sheet upside for less — the same logic that makes Guéhi at £6.0m attractive when City's early fixtures are easy (Bournemouth, Palace, Coventry).
- The £5.0m bracket is this season's sweet spot for defensive spending: Vuskovic (Brighton, club-record £46m signing, "as nailed as it gets" as Van Hecke's replacement), Ballard (Sunderland), Canvot (Palace), Botman (Newcastle), Maguire (Man Utd, early-fixture punt), N.Williams (Forest — most attempts and shots on target among defenders last season, but Forest had the league's highest xGC).
- Ajer (Brentford £4.5m) is the community's standout cheap starter — "a no-brainer if Van den Berg is out long-term" — alongside Kayode (Brentford £4.5m), Hume (Sunderland £4.5m), Shaw (Man Utd £4.5m, fixtures good but the defence is criticised), Boscagli and De Cuyper (Brighton £4.5m).

## The budget bracket (£4.0–4.5m)

- Defence is where the best budget options live, and 2026/27 has an unusually deep £4.0m pool among the promoted clubs: Hull (Hughes, Egan, Mendy, Herrington), Coventry (Thomas, van Ewijk), Ipswich (Diop, O'Shea).
- For promoted-club defenders, **chase the DefCon floor, not clean sheets**: promoted teams' clean-sheet odds are a lottery (Burnley went from 30 Championship shut-outs to 4 in the Premier League), while a nailed £4.0m defender hitting 10+ defensive actions banks DefCon points most weeks. The minus-points worry is overrated — defenders lose only 1 point per two goals conceded, so a +2 DefCon defender needs four goals against to be cancelled out.
- Ownership caution: the two most-owned £4.0m defenders (Diop 18.4%, van Ewijk 15.3%) are price-drop candidates — price moves with the volume of managers buying or selling, and high ownership means more sellers and fewer future buyers.
- Bench policy: a 4.0 defender is the best bench fodder in the game (cheaper than equivalent mids/forwards), which is the counter-argument to loading up on expensive defenders — see below.

## Big at the back

- "Big at the back" — five starting defenders, several at £5.0m+, funded by a cheap bench and a tanked forward slot — is a live structure debate for 2026/27. The case: exceptional value in the new £5.0m centre-backs, Arsenal's defensive quality, strong City and United fixtures, and the reliability of Virgil/Tarkowski-type picks. If you go big at the back, Mbeumo-style midfielders are considered mandatory to keep attacking cover.
- The counter-cases: (a) 4.0 defenders are better and cheaper bench fillers than their mid/forward equivalents, so a 5-at-the-back team accumulates dead spots and bench money; (b) fitting Haaland, Bruno Fernandes and João Pedro alongside a big back five is very hard; (c) the compromise structure is a "big 4" — four strong starters plus one cheap £4.0–4.5m fifth defender and a playing bench.
- Rotation is the natural middle ground: five playing defenders rotated by fixture (4.5s from solid defences) captures clean sheets without the premium spend — the classic "rotation" defence, supported by the fixture ticker's clean-sheet percentages.

## When to prioritise defence

- Spend on defence early when fixtures favour it (Everton, Man City, United's opening runs), and rebalance when fixture swings arrive — defensive picks bought for a clean-sheet run need an exit plan like any other asset.
- DefCon-heavy defenders lower variance: they are the "floor" picks of a team built for consistent points rather than hauls. If a squad needs safety (Bench Boost weeks, mini-league leads), DefCon defenders are the place to find it.
- Do not overpay for clean-sheet certainty: clean sheets are far harder to predict than DefCon output, and the premium defenders' advantage narrows once the underlying defensive quality is available cheaper (see the Arsenal £5.5m route above).
