# 2026/27 Early Season — After Gameweek 1

Timely document describing where the 2026/27 season stands after Gameweek 1: results, prices, the players being bought and sold, and what published community opinion is saying. It replaces the pre-season consensus document, whose validity window ended on 21 August 2026.

**Every number here is FPL's own data read on 25 August 2026**, and the community section is published opinion from the same week, named and dated. Ownership, transfer counts and prices move daily — **a price in this document is a historical fact about 25 August, not a current price.** When it disagrees with a tool, the tool wins. This document ages out on 20 September; after that the reference documents carry the strategy and the tools carry the numbers.

## What Gameweek 1 actually was

Results: Arsenal 3–0 Coventry, Hull 2–0 Man Utd, Everton 2–0 Crystal Palace, Ipswich 2–1 Sunderland, Nott'm Forest 0–1 Leeds, Brentford 3–0 Spurs, Brighton 4–0 Aston Villa, Man City 2–1 Bournemouth, Newcastle 2–2 Liverpool, Fulham 2–3 Chelsea.

- The gameweek's best returns came almost entirely from **outside the template**: De Cuyper (Brighton, £4.5m defender) 17 points, Hinshelwood (Brighton) 16, Mendy (Hull, £4.0m) 15, Sangaré (Brentford, £5.5m) 14, Ajayi (Hull, £4.0m) 14, Kayode (Brentford, £4.5m) 13.
- The most-owned premiums largely did not return: Haaland 2 points at 68.4% ownership, B.Fernandes 2 at 49.5%, Mbeumo 2 at 36.8%, Semenyo 2 at 25.0%.
- The template picks that did deliver: João Pedro (Chelsea) 11 points at 66.1% ownership, Palmer 13, Ødegaard 11, Calafiori 9, Szoboszlai 8.

## What one gameweek is worth as evidence

This is the single most important framing for advice given right now.

- **One match is not form.** A 17-point defender has one data point; the season-long numbers the tools report will not exist in useful volume for several more gameweeks. Advice built on GW1 alone is advice built on noise.
- The reference documents, the underlying-stat tools (xG, xGA) and last season's data are the better evidence base until roughly GW5–6. Weight them accordingly, and say which season a number comes from.
- **A blanking premium is not a broken premium.** Haaland on 2 points after one match tells you nothing his price does not already price in; selling a 68%-owned premium after one blank is the single most expensive knee-jerk in the game.
- The exception to all of this is **information, not points**: who started, who was benched, who came off injured, and which promoted clubs looked competitive. That is real and usable after one match.

## Prices: nothing has moved yet

- **As of 25 August, not a single player's price has changed this season** — FPL's own
  `cost_change_start` and `cost_change_event` fields read zero for all 700-odd players.
- The mechanism is live: prices were locked until the GW1 deadline (Friday 21 August, 18:30 BST)
  and changes have been enabled since. For 2026/27 they land at **midnight UK time**, moved from
  the old ~01:30 GMT / 02:30 BST cut-off, and FPL now publishes its own **Price Change Predictor**
  showing each player's percentage progress toward a rise or fall.
- What that means practically: the heavy GW1 buying below has not been paid for yet. A manager
  moving now still buys at the starting price, and the first wave of rises is the thing that
  ends that window. Team value earned in the opening weeks is the cheapest team value of the
  season.
- Do not state a price from this document. Prices move daily from here; the tools carry today's.

## Closest to a price rise

FPL moves a price on **net transfers relative to how many people already own the player**, so a
cheap, lightly-owned player who is being bought hard rises long before a heavily-owned premium
does. Ranking by net transfers in as a share of current owners, on 25 August (9.67m managers):

- **De Cuyper** (Brighton, £4.5m DEF) — 313k net in against 5.8% ownership. The most-bought player
  in the game and the clearest rise candidate.
- **Hinshelwood** (Brighton, £6.0m MID) — 89k net in from 1.9% ownership.
- **Sangaré** (Brentford, £5.5m MID) — 252k net in from 6.5%.
- **Kayode** (Brentford, £4.5m DEF) — 197k net in from 6.0%.
- **Mendy** (Hull, £4.0m DEF) and **Tzolakis** (Hull, £4.5m GK) — the cheap end of the Hull
  bandwagon after their win over Man Utd.
- Also under pressure from a small base: **Janelt** (Brentford), **King** (Fulham), **Emersonn**
  (Ipswich), **Bijol** (Leeds), **Elanga** (Newcastle).

The premiums being bought heavily — João Pedro (201k net), Calafiori (200k), Ødegaard (172k),
Palmer (156k) — are moving in absolute terms but sit on much larger ownership, so they rise more
slowly. That is why the cheap defender always rises first.

## Closest to a price fall

Same arithmetic in reverse. Heaviest net sales on 25 August:

- **Pedro Porro** (Spurs, £5.5m) — 256k net out after Brentford beat Spurs 3–0.
- **Gyökeres** (Arsenal, £7.5m) — 142k net out, no return in GW1.
- **Gibbs-White** (Nott'm Forest, £8.0m) — 136k net out.
- **Konsa** (Arsenal, £4.5m) — 128k net out; **Watkins** (Aston Villa, £8.0m) — 128k out after the
  4–0 at Brighton.
- **B.Fernandes** (Man Utd, £12.0m) — 110k net out, still 49.4% owned. A player this widely owned
  falls slowly, but the pressure is real.
- Small-ownership players falling fastest as a share of owners: **Spence**, **Romero** and
  **P.M.Sarr** (Spurs), **Doku** (Man City), **Sarr** (Crystal Palace).

A fall costs you nothing directly — you only lose money if you sell after it — but it does mean a
player you are holding is losing the value you would need to buy back in later.

## Hot players after Gameweek 1

Where the points and the buying overlap:

- **De Cuyper** (£4.5m) — 17 points from a goal, an assist, a clean sheet and two bonus, in
  Brighton's 4–0 win. The gameweek's outstanding return at the cheapest possible price.
- **Sangaré** (£5.5m) — 14 points against Spurs, and Brentford's 3–0 came with Kayode's 13 beside
  it. Brentford's whole cheap end is in demand.
- **Palmer** (£9.5m) — a goal and an assist in 82 minutes at Fulham, 13 points.
- **Ødegaard** (£6.5m) and **Calafiori** (£5.5m) — Arsenal's 3–0 produced returns at both ends of
  the pitch, and Arsenal players occupy three of the eight most-bought slots.
- **Tzolis** (£6.5m) — already 25.8% owned, and reported as the beneficiary of Trossard's
  departure.
- **Hull's cheap defence** — Mendy (£4.0m) 15 points and Ajayi (£4.0m) 14 in the win over Man Utd.
  Two 4.0 defenders returning double figures is the sort of thing that reshapes budgets, and the
  sort of thing that does not repeat.

## Who the market is selling, and whether it should

The transfers-out column is Porro, Gyökeres, Gibbs-White, Konsa, Watkins, Rice and Bruno
Guimarães — mostly players who blanked once, at clubs that lost once.

- **Selling a premium after one blank is the most expensive habit in FPL.** Haaland (2 points,
  68.4% owned), B.Fernandes (2, 49.4%) and Mbeumo (2, 36.8%) all underperformed their price in
  GW1. Their fixtures, minutes and underlying numbers have not changed in one week.
- The defensible sales are the ones with a *reason beyond the score*: a player who lost their
  starting place, picked up a knock, or moved club. Ask which of those applies before advising a
  move.
- The bandwagon itself is a cost. A £4.5m defender bought at 5.8% ownership after a 17-point
  haul is being bought at the top of his price and the top of his form.
