# Set-and-Forget and Low-Maintenance FPL

Reference document for managers who cannot or do not want to check FPL every day: what set-and-forget actually means, which squad slots absorb it well and which do not, how to build a squad that survives inattention, and the three decisions that still cannot be skipped. Written for 2026/27 scoring and chip rules.

## What set-and-forget means, and what it does not

- Set-and-forget is a *slot-level* decision, not a whole-team philosophy. It means picking a player for a slot and leaving them there for months — no rotation, no fixture-by-fixture switching — so the slot costs no attention and no transfers.
- It does not mean never transferring. Even the most hands-off manager still reacts to a long-term injury, a player leaving the club, or a starter losing their place. What set-and-forget removes is the *weekly optional* decision, which is where most transfers and most hits go.
- The trade is explicit: you give up the points a well-timed rotation would have earned in exchange for never losing points to a badly timed one, and for getting your week back.

## Why it usually wins for a casual manager

- Most transfers are not free money. A 4-point hit needs the incoming player to outscore the outgoing one by more than four over the horizon you actually hold them, and the evidence across seasons is that routine hits lose points on average.
- Knee-jerk moves are the main leak. A player blanks once, gets transferred out, and hauls the following week for someone else — a pattern that costs a transfer, sometimes a hit, and the points themselves.
- Attention is a real constraint. A manager who checks once a week before the deadline will make worse rotation calls than one who watches every press conference, so a strategy that assumes daily attention is a strategy that will be executed badly.

## Which slots absorb set-and-forget well

- **Goalkeeper.** The cheapest slot to leave alone: a keeper at a defensively solid club plays every week, and the ceiling difference between keepers is small. A single £4.5–5.5m starter plus a £4.0m bench keeper is the standard low-maintenance shape. See `strategy-goalkeepers.md` for the pool.
- **Premium attackers.** A £12m+ midfielder or forward at a top club plays in almost every fixture and is captained often; benching or selling them by fixture is where casual managers lose most.
- **Nailed defenders with a second scoring route.** A starting defender who also takes corners, carries a goal threat or racks up Defensive Contributions scores in bad fixtures too, which is exactly what a slot you never touch needs. See `strategy-defence-and-clean-sheets.md`.

## Which slots do not absorb it

- **Rotation-risk midfielders and forwards at squad-depth clubs.** A player who starts two games in three will blank in a way no fixture ticker predicted, and the loss compounds because you are not watching.
- **£4.0–4.5m bench outfielders.** They are enablers, not points. Do not expect a bench slot to become set-and-forget value; expect it to be a body who is fit and starts, so autosubs can work.
- **Anyone at a club in a manager change or a relegation fight.** The starting XI moves under a new manager, and the news arrives faster than a weekly check.

## Building a squad that survives inattention

- **Prefer nailed over exciting.** For a low-maintenance squad, minutes certainty is worth more than upside: a 5-point floor every week beats a 12-point ceiling that appears once in five.
- **Keep a playing bench.** Four fit players who start, not four £4.0m benchwarmers. Autosubs only rescue a blank if the substitute actually played, and a playing bench is what makes an unchecked deadline survivable.
- **Set the vice-captain seriously, and set the bench order.** These are the two settings that work *while you are not there*. A vice-captain at a different club to the captain, and a bench ordered by who is most likely to play, are free insurance.
- **Spread club risk.** Three players from one club is one manager's rotation decision away from a bad week you did not see coming.
- **Bank the flexibility, not the transfer.** Rolling a transfer gives you two next week, which is what turns a surprise injury into a fix rather than a hit.

## The three decisions you still cannot skip

1. **Captain.** The single biggest weekly swing in the game, and no default survives the season. Even a set-and-forget manager should give this thirty seconds: the premium with the best fixture, or the most-owned premium if you are protecting a rank. See `captain-selection-and-triple-captain.md`.
2. **Availability.** Check flags before the deadline. A flagged player left in the XI is a guaranteed zero that a thirty-second check would have caught, and the tools here answer it directly.
3. **Chips.** Chips expire. A wildcard unplayed by the end of its window is worth nothing, and the second-half set does not carry the first-half one over. See `strategy-chips.md`.

## The lower-maintenance version of chips

- **Wildcard on a schedule, not on a whim.** Around GW8–9 the first-half wildcard usually has enough information behind it (price rises banked, the template settled, injuries known) without needing weekly monitoring.
- **Bench Boost and Triple Captain in a double gameweek.** They are the two chips that pay for themselves with no ongoing management — pick the double, play the chip. See `strategy-blank-and-double-gameweeks.md`.
- **Free Hit is the highest-maintenance chip**, because its value depends on a squad state that changes week to week. A low-maintenance manager can reasonably save it for a blank gameweek and no other reason.

## Common failure modes

- **Set-and-forget used as a defence of a broken squad.** If a player has lost their place or moved club, the slot is not low-maintenance; it is dead. Leaving it is not the strategy, it is neglect.
- **Mixing rotation and set-and-forget in the same slot pair.** Buying a rotation partner and then never rotating wastes the money twice.
- **Assuming the fixture ticker replaces attention.** A ticker predicts opponent difficulty, not team news. Fixtures are the input to a *transfer* decision, not to whether a player is playing this weekend.
