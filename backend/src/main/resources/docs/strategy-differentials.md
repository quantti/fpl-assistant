# FPL Differentials

Reference document on differential players: definition, ownership tiers, why they matter, how to find them, and when they are worth owning.

## Definition

A differential is a player owned by a small percentage of FPL managers. There is no official cut-off; community tiers:

- Heavy differential: <3% owned.
- Strong differential: 3–7% owned.
- Soft differential: 7–15% owned.
- Template: 15%+ owned (the crowd already owns him).

Lower ownership = higher rank impact on a haul, and bigger rank loss on a blank. The trade-off is the whole game: differential strategy is being right when the crowd is wrong, not being contrarian for its own sake.

## Why they matter (the maths)

- A template-only squad can only gain rank by executing slightly better (captaincy, bench order, fewer injuries) — a few hundred ranks per week at best.
- One differential haul can jump ~50,000 ranks in a single Gameweek: a 12-point haul from a 4%-owned player out-scores 96% of the field.
- Elite managers (top 1k/10k) typically run 1–2 calculated differentials at all times — not five, not zero.
- Five differentials is reckless (too many compounding small bets). Zero is timid (can only hold rank, never gain it).

## Types of differential

1. **Form-based**: quietly outperforming ownership — returns from non-obvious sources (assists, bonus, defensive contributions) on a less glamorous team. Underlying data (xG, xA, shots on target) differs from the casual perception.
2. **Fixture-based**: team entering a run of favourable fixtures most managers have not spotted. The edge is the fixture swing; the window closes once price rises and ownership catches up.
3. **Role-based**: role recently improved — new manager, teammate injury, tactical switch, set-piece promotion. Role changes take 2–3 Gameweeks to show in stats; ownership lags behind.
4. **Returnee-based**: returning from injury/international duty with cratered ownership; strong underlying numbers from earlier plus a clear role make the first weeks back a hidden-gem window (the crowd is slow to re-buy).

## Checklist for a genuine differential

Pass all five → real differential. Pass four → watch-list. Pass three or fewer → a punt (and punts tank seasons).

1. **Strong underlying numbers**: xG, xA, shots on target, big chances above what ownership implies; look for the gap between doing and scoring.
2. **Minutes reliability**: 70+ minutes most games, ideally 80+; a starter who only plays every other week is not a differential. These are strategic preferences, not forecast probabilities. The primary ranking requires fresh available status and complete same-season, current-club evidence; minutes feed its projection rather than acting as a 60+ admission gate. It excludes for minutes only when the three latest complete observed fixtures are all below 60, including zero-minute absences. Short or mixed windows remain scored at their observed rate but are labelled small samples; stale or incomplete evidence means abstention, and no record guarantees the next start. See `strategy-minutes-and-rotation.md`.
3. **Favourable upcoming fixtures**: a 4-Gameweek run rated ~2 or better defensively; rollercoaster fixtures = coin flip.
4. **Clear role**: set-piece taker, penalty taker, primary creator, goal-scoring forward — a defined route to points, not "might pop up with a goal".
5. **A reason for low ownership**: small team nobody watches (green light) vs benched twice last month (red flag). Missing this step is the most common error.

## When they are worth it

- **Worth it when chasing**: outside top 50k or trailing a mini-league; only differentials create separation.
- **Not worth it when protecting**: inside top 50k with a small lead; safe, high-ownership picks preserve rank.
- Ownership matters less for captaincy than fixture quality: a 5%-owned differential is a good squad pick but only a captain option if the fixture and ceiling justify it.

## Common mistakes

- Confusing "low owned" with "differential": a bad player with low ownership is just a bad player.
- Differentials without the five-point checklist: no route to points, no minutes, no fixture basis.
- Running too many at once; compounding small bets across the squad.
- Differential-captaining without a fixture/ceiling justification.
- Ignoring the "why is he low-owned" question (red-flag reasons: rotation, injury, lost role).
