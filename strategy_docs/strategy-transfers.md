# FPL Transfer Strategy

Reference document on FPL transfer management: free transfer rules, when to roll, when to take hits, and long-term planning.

## Transfer system rules

- One free transfer (FT) per Gameweek, first available after the GW1 deadline.
- Unused FT rolls over, max five banked. Once at the cap of 5, playing a Gameweek without using a transfer gains nothing further — you stay capped at 5, you do not lose transfers you already hold.
- Additional transfers cost -4 points each ("taking a hit"). E.g. three transfers with one FT = -8.
- Wildcard and Free Hit allow unlimited transfers for one Gameweek without deductions.
- Transfer decisions apply to the upcoming Gameweek deadline.

## Rolling transfers

Rolling is underrated; banked transfers are the cheapest flexibility in the game.

**Roll when:**
- Squad is in good shape: all 15 fit, playing regularly, reasonable fixtures.
- No clear upgrade exists; a forced transfer for its own sake is a waste.
- A fixture swing is coming in 2–3 Gameweeks; two FTs let you make two moves without a hit.
- International break context: injury risk and uncertain team news argue for waiting.
- Key player's injury prognosis unclear; waiting for press conferences beats guessing.

**Value of patience:** rolling in GW10 gives two FTs in GW11, equivalent to saving 4 points vs taking a hit. Disciplined rolling can save 20–30 points over a season.

## When to use the free transfer

- **Injury replacement**: confirmed injury to a starter missing multiple GWs → replace immediately. Exception: injured player is benched with adequate cover.
- **Fixture swings**: bring in the premium whose fixtures turn favourable before a price rise; sell before a difficult run.
- **Form changes**: sustained underperformance over 4–5 GWs justifies selling. One or two blanks is variance, not a crisis.
- **Price change management**: buy before a rise if you otherwise cannot afford; sell before a drop to protect squad value. Never let price changes force a transfer you would not otherwise make.

## Taking hits: when justified

A hit is justified when expected return from the new player exceeds the sold player's expected return plus the -4 cost.

**Justified:**
- Replacing a non-playing starter when bench cover is poor: replacement needs to outscore zero by >4 points — a low bar.
- DGW targeting: a player with two fixtures has roughly double the expected points of a single-fixture player.
- Restructuring for a fixture swing: one hit fixing two problems can beat two separate moves.
- Captain target acquisition: expected doubled captain return minus 4 still beats the alternative captain.

**Not justified:**
- Sideways moves: e.g. one 6.5m midfielder for another because the new one scored last week. Needs to outscore the old one by 5+ points to break even.
- Chasing last week's points: past returns do not guarantee future returns.
- Panic transfers after a bad Gameweek: a -8 or -12 after one poor week almost never pays.
- Early-week hits: making hit transfers early in the week before team news adds avoidable risk.

## Long-term planning

- Plan transfers 4–6 Gameweeks ahead using fixture difficulty ratings.
- Align transfers with upcoming fixture swings rather than reacting week-to-week.
- Use banked transfers to handle predictable events (blank Gameweeks, DGWs, cup congestion) without hits.
- Distinguish variance from systemic problems before restructuring.

## Decision summary

| Situation | Action |
|---|---|
| Squad healthy, no upgrade | Roll the transfer |
| Starter injured (multi-GW) | Replace immediately |
| Fixture swing incoming | Plan 2 transfers ahead, avoid hits |
| Non-playing starter, weak bench | Hit justified (-4) |
| DGW player acquisition | Hit justified |
| Sideways move / chasing points | Do not take the hit |
| Bad Gameweek panic | Hold, do not take -8/-12 |
