# FPL Player Archetypes

Reference document on recurring player categories ("archetypes") in FPL squad building. The term archetype is borrowed from psychology/game design (a recurring role template); in FPL sources the same idea appears as player profiles, price tiers and roles. This document defines the standard categories used to evaluate and structure a squad.

## Archetype overview

An archetype is a recurring player profile with characteristic price, role, ownership and expected-points behaviour. Squad building consists of selecting a mix of archetypes: premiums provide ceiling, enablers fund them, value picks outperform their price, differentials create rank separation.

## The archetypes

### Premium / talisman
- Definition: elite, expensive player (e.g. Haaland ~£14m+, Salah) who is the centrepiece of a top team's attack; the default captaincy candidate.
- Identification: top underlying data (xG, xA, bonus points, BPS), high ownership (50%+), near-guaranteed minutes when fit, often penalty duty.
- Role in squad: main points engine and captaincy anchor; 2–3 premiums is the standard structure.
- Risks: high cost forces cheap elsewhere; rotation in congested periods; form droughts are costly.

### Mid-priced
- Definition: £6.5–8.5m players with solid output; the flexible middle of the squad.
- Role: rotate in/out on fixtures, occasional differential captain, injured-premium replacement.
- Risks: the "tweener" trap — costs premium money without premium ceiling; sideways transfer temptation.

### Budget enabler
- Definition: ≤£5.5m player who starts regularly and has a route to attacking/clean-sheet points; frees budget for premiums.
- Identification: guaranteed starter, attacking upside (even 1–2 returns/month), defensively solid team (clean-sheet reliability for defenders), ≤5.5m.
- Role: fills the cheap slots (fifth midfielder, third defender, bench) without dead weight.
- Risks: minutes uncertainty at big clubs (rotation); the true enabler starts every week — a bench enabler only covers gaps.

### Value pick
- Definition: any price, but points-per-million (PPM) above elite thresholds — roughly 25+ PPM defenders, 22+ midfielders, 20+ forwards.
- Identification: total points ÷ current price; look for output exceeding the price tag, often via non-obvious returns (set pieces, bonus, defensive contributions).
- Role: fund the premium structure; value picks, not price tags, win mini-leagues.
- Risks: value is contextual — fixture runs and role changes shift it; a value pick is not automatically a keeper.

### Differential
- Definition: low-ownership player (<15%, ideally <7%) with strong underlying data, minutes, fixtures and a defined role.
- Role: rank separation when chasing; 1–2 per squad max.
- See the dedicated differentials reference document for the full framework.

### Set-and-forget
- Definition: a player bought for a long window (often a whole season) whose ownership does not require weekly attention — typically the premium talisman or a fixture-proof defensive asset.
- Identification: minutes security, team strength, fixtures irrelevant to keep/transfer decision.
- Role: reduces transfer workload and hit exposure.
- Risks: opportunity cost if the meta shifts (injury, role change, new signing).

### Rotation / bench asset
- Definition: cheapish player rotated in based on fixtures (e.g. 4.5–5.5m defender facing weak attacks).
- Role: bench coverage, fixture-based starts, occasional clean-sheet/bonus upside.
- Risks: benched unexpectedly; only useful with a working auto-sub order and fixture awareness.

## Squad structure implications

- Standard competitive structure: 2–3 premiums + value picks + budget enablers; avoid 15 mid-price players (no ceiling) and 5+ punts (no floor).
- Fund premiums ruthlessly via enablers; hunt value everywhere else.
- Archetype mix should match game state: protecting a lead → template-heavy (premiums, set-and-forget); chasing → add differentials.
