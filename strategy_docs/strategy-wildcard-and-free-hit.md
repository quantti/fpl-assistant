# FPL Wildcard and Free Hit Strategy

Reference document on the Wildcard (WC) and Free Hit (FH) chips: when and how to play them, and how they combine with Double Gameweeks (DGW), Blank Gameweeks (BGW) and Bench Boost (BB).

## The two flexibility chips

Two chip sets run this season: GW1–19 and GW20–38, each with its own Wildcard and Free Hit.

- **Wildcard (×2/season, one per set)**: unlimited free transfers for one Gameweek; the new squad is permanent. WC1 must be used by the GW19 deadline or it is lost — it does not carry into the second set. WC2 is available GW20–38.
- **Free Hit (×2/season, one per set)**: unlimited free transfers for one Gameweek; the squad reverts afterwards. A one-week chip, not a rebuild. FH1 (GW1–19) is likewise lost if unused by the GW19 deadline.
- Both are active until the Gameweek deadline; changes can be made and reversed freely before it. Player prices can still change while either chip is active.
- Distinction: WC changes the squad permanently (structure reset); FH maximises a single week without touching long-term structure.

## Scheduling foundation: blanks and doubles

Chip timing is driven by the schedule:

- **DGW**: a team plays twice in one Gameweek, created when postponed fixtures (FA Cup, Carabao Cup, European congestion) are rescheduled. DGWs cluster in the second half, mainly GW25–37, with the largest in GW34–37.
- **BGW**: several teams have no fixture in a Gameweek (the flip side of cup postponements); BGWs typically precede DGWs.
- **Planning**: track cup progress (the best predictor of future blanks/doubles), follow official fixture announcements (confirmed 2–4 weeks ahead), and use community planners. Keep chip decisions flexible until schedules are confirmed.

## Wildcard strategy

### When to play WC1 (GW1–19)

Common windows:
- **GW4–6 early adjustment**: correct initial-squad mistakes, react to early fixture swings, buy underpriced players before price rises, reshape structure if the meta shifted. Risk: small sample size.
- **GW7–10 international-break reset**: assess international injuries, quarter-season data sample, clearer rotation patterns, fixture difficulty shifts.
- **GW12–16 fixture swing**: last comfortable window before the GW19 deadline; loads up on teams with kind festive-period fixtures (congested schedule rewards consistent starters).

Whatever the trigger, WC1 must be played by the GW19 deadline — an unused Wildcard does not carry into the second set.

WC1 is a mistake when: the squad can be fixed with one or two free transfers; problems are short-term injuries; reacting emotionally to one bad Gameweek; no clear target players yet.

### When to play WC2 (GW20–38)

- **Before a major DGW**: stack players with two fixtures; especially potent combined with Bench Boost (15 players all with doubles).
- **After a BGW**: rebuild specifically for the run-in after navigating the blank with Free Hit.
- **Final push (GW33–36)**: tailors the squad to remaining fixtures, motivation (relegation/Europe battles), and late-season form.
- WC2 is usually the strategic centrepiece of the second half; post-GW20 the season is settled enough to plan blanks/doubles confidently.

### Building a Wildcard squad

- Plan the next 4–8 Gameweeks, not just the next one.
- Start from the template: understand why high-owned picks are popular; the template protects rank while differentials are chosen deliberately.
- Include a set-and-forget spine: secure minutes, strong role, long-term fixture appeal, good value — players to hold through good and average weeks.
- Target fixture swings: buy 2–3 assets from teams entering a good run; sell teams entering a poor run; map six Gameweeks of fixtures.
- Protect structure: can you move between premiums with one transfer? Is there a route to expensive players (price ceiling)? Does the bench cover rotation?
- Bench matters even without Bench Boost: at least one or two usable bench spots for busy schedules.
- Template vs differential on WC: template for rank protection/first half; differential-heavy WC2 when chasing late.

### Wildcard mistakes

- Wildcarding too early out of frustration (noisy early data).
- Chasing last week's points instead of underlying stats (xG, xA, minutes, role).
- Ignoring fixture runs (six+ fixtures per pick).
- Neglecting squad depth (cheapest possible bench).
- Ignoring team value/price ceiling (no pathway to premiums later).
- Using WC when 1–2 free transfers would solve the problem.

## Free Hit strategy

Two Free Hits are available: FH1 (GW1–19, lost if unused by the GW19 deadline) and FH2 (GW20–38). DGWs only occur GW25–37, so FH1 almost never targets a double — it is a GW1–19 tool for blanks and fixture spikes, while FH2 is the one that can chase a DGW.

### When to play

- **BGW (classic use)**: field a full XI when the regular squad would have only 8–10 active players; keeps long-term structure intact instead of selling/rebuying blanking assets.
- **DGW (FH2 only — DGWs fall in GW25–37)**: load up on the best double-Gameweek players and stack a premium captain with two fixtures. Only worth it if the current squad lacks good double coverage and the doubles are strong (quality fixtures, low rotation risk).
- **Sharp fixture spike**: one Gameweek where a few top teams have excellent fixtures and the regular squad is poorly set up. Only for a short spike, not a fixture run (transfers/WC handle runs).

### How to maximise a Free Hit

- Budget matters less: build for one deadline, not long-term value. Aggressively stack premiums with doubles or elite home fixtures.
- Build for ceiling: DGW attackers with penalty/set-piece roles, defenders with strong clean-sheet odds, high-upside captain/vice-captain, explosive differentials.
- Ignore long-term structure: no need for bench depth, safe long-term holds, or money in the bank.
- Push limits creatively: triple-ups on the best teams and defensive stacking are optimal here (formation, budget and 3-per-club limits still apply).

### Free Hit decision framework

- How many players would field without the chip? Struggling to reach 10–11 in a BGW → FH attractive.
- How far is the current squad from the ideal one-week team? Bigger gap = better chip value.
- Are there premiums with doubles/elite fixtures unreachable otherwise? If yes, FH gains value.
- Would normal transfers solve most of it? If yes, save the chip.
- Captaincy: the FH should clearly improve the captain pick; the biggest FH gains often come from landing the best premium captain.

### Free Hit mistakes

- Saving it too long for a perfect week that never comes (unused chips have no value).
- Burning it on a normal Gameweek when transfers suffice.
- Overrating weak doubles: two mediocre fixtures are not automatically better than one elite fixture.
- Neglecting captaincy improvement.
- Using it emotionally rather than for a structural edge.

## Bench Boost interplay

- Two Bench Boosts are available, one per chip set (BB1 in GW1–19, BB2 in GW20–38); BB1 can never target a DGW since DGWs only start GW25 — BB1's best week is simply the strongest all-15-starting fixture list within GW1–19, while BB2 is the one built around a DGW.
- BB counts all 15 players' points; its value depends entirely on a playable bench (four strong bench picks, not cheap non-players).
- Best BB2 week: a DGW where all 15 are expected to start, with favourable fixtures and low rotation risk. A bench with four starters in a DGW can add 15–30+ points.
- Build toward BB over several transfers (upgrade dead bench spots, prioritise minutes over upside on the bench, plan backward to the target week), not in the deadline week.
- Save BB when: bench has non-starters/injury doubts; reaching four decent bench players requires multiple hits; the target Gameweek has heavy rotation risk or poor fixtures.

## Combined sequencing (the second chip set, GW20–38)

Classic chip sequence around blanks and doubles:

1. **Identify** BGW/DGW windows early (cup progress, planners); keep chips unplayed until schedules confirm.
2. **WC2 → BB**: Wildcard into a 15-man squad with strong minutes and double coverage, then Bench Boost in the following DGW. The strongest chip combination in FPL.
3. **FH for the BGW**: use Free Hit to field a full XI in the blank without breaking the structure built for the DGW; the squad reverts, ready for the double.
4. **Remaining chips** (TC, BB if not yet used) slot into the strongest doubles; plan the whole sequence as one season-long sequence, not per-week decisions.
5. Order matters: playing WC too early can leave injuries/rotation damaging the BB window; playing FH too late wastes its one-week rescue value.

## Decision summary

| Situation | Chip |
|---|---|
| Multiple squad problems, fixture swing, structure reset | Wildcard |
| 1–2 fixes needed | Free transfers, save chips |
| BGW leaves 8–10 active players | Free Hit |
| Strong DGW, squad lacks double coverage | Free Hit (one week) |
| Strong DGW + plan to maximise 15 players | Wildcard → Bench Boost |
| Weak bench, no doubles | Do not play BB; fix bench first |
| Emotional reaction to one bad week | Do not wildcard |
