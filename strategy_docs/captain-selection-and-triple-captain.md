# FPL Captain Selection and the Triple Captain Chip

Reference document on FPL captaincy theory and the Triple Captain (TC) chip: selection criteria, strategies, historical cases and failure modes.

## Captaincy: general principles

- The captain doubles one player's points (×2) every Gameweek; the Triple Captain chip triples them (×3) for one Gameweek.
- Captaincy is the highest-leverage recurring decision in FPL. Chips are one-off; the armband applies every week.
- A wrong captain pick can cost hundreds of points over a season; a wasted chip can derail it.

## Captain selection criteria

**Player profile**
- Premium player: the captain should be the team's undisputed talisman (e.g. Salah, Haaland, Isak, Palmer). Cheap players rarely have the ceiling for a haul.
- Secure minutes: near-certain starter; rotation risk kills captaincy.
- Penalty duty: adds hidden upside independent of open play.
- Attacking role: goals, assists, shots, big chances, bonus potential.
- Underlying data: xG, xA, minutes, home/away splits, opponent's xG conceded.

**Fixtures and matchups**
- Fixture difficulty is the single most important factor, especially early season when form data is stale.
- Best targets: weak defences, promoted sides, teams conceding many big chances.
- Home advantage: roughly +0.3 expected goals per match; matters when comparing near-equal players.
- Double Gameweek (DGW): two matches in one Gameweek; two opportunities for the captain.

**Safe vs differential (depends on game state)**
- Safe: high-ownership premium; moves in line with the field, minimises variance. Use when protecting a lead / inside top 50k.
- Differential: 10–35% owned player with a good fixture and high ceiling; the only way to gain ground. Use when chasing / outside top 50k.
- A high-ownership captain (e.g. Haaland ~74% ownership) provides no relative edge.
- Top managers vary captaincy by player and league position; casual players pick the same captain weekly.

**Psychological traps**
- Loss aversion: choosing the popular captain out of fear of regret; if everyone captains him, no relative gain.
- Recency bias: Gameweek form is noisy small-sample data; early season, judge team quality and matchups, not last season's stats.
- Availability bias: captaining the most-discussed name rather than the analytically best pick.
- Golden rule: process > outcome. A sound pick can still fail (missed penalty, early sub, red card, outstanding goalkeeper performance).

## Triple Captain chip

**Rules**
- One TC per season (some seasons award two, one per half; check official rules).
- Captain points ×3 for a single Gameweek; activated from the Pick Team tab before deadline.

**When to play**
- Default rule: premium player in a DGW, ideally two easy home fixtures.
- DGW raises the floor and keeps the ceiling high; two chances at goals, assists, appearance points and bonus.
- DGWs historically cluster in GW25–37 (largest GW34–37), caused by FA Cup, Carabao Cup and European rescheduling.
- A single-Gameweek play is justified when: player in unstoppable form (eye test + data); opponent vulnerable (big chances, set pieces, transitions); player has huge ceiling; DGW alternatives flawed (rotation, poor fixtures, low upside).
- Famous single-Gameweek case: Salah TC vs Watford 2021/22 (72 points from a 24-point haul).

**Who to pick**
- Never: uncertain starters, rotation risks, players carrying knocks.
- Avoid: picks justified only by "two fixtures" without role, minutes, team quality and matchup support.
- Pre-activation checks: injury news, manager interviews, secure minutes in both DGW matches, penalty duty, team attacking output.

**Pre-activation checklist**
- Does the captain have a DGW? Will he play both matches?
- Will a better opportunity arise later (2 × easy DGW beats 1 × easy DGW)?
- Is he a secure starter, fully fit, on penalties?
- Are the fixtures actually good, or merely "two games"?
- Can the rank situation afford the risk (safe vs differential)?
- Confirm team news and deadline.

## Historical TC outcomes

**Best TC decisions**
- Salah 2021/22 vs Watford (away): 5–0 win, 24 points → 72 on TC. Single Gameweek; elite form + weak opponent outweighed DGW edge.
- Haaland 2022/23 DGW (Brighton + Man United at home): premium, two home games, elite numbers, secure penalties; the modern template.
- Other iconic hits (Agüero, Kane, Salah) share: centrepiece of the attack, near-guaranteed minutes, penalties, path to goals and bonus.

**Common denominators of successful TC calls**
1. Premium player.
2. Centrepiece of the team's attack.
3. Penalty duty.
4. Strong form.
5. Fixture (one or two matches) with both floor and ceiling.

**Why TC flops (common causes)**
1. Rotation: starts only one DGW match or benched unexpectedly.
2. Fitness risk: reduced minutes from a minor injury.
3. Overrating the DGW: two average fixtures are not automatically better than one elite fixture.
4. Ignoring context: tough opponents, fixture congestion, tactical changes.
5. Randomness: missed penalty, early sub, red card, goalkeeper's outstanding display.

## Decision framework (summary)

| Question | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| TC default | Premium player in a DGW, ideally two easy home fixtures. |
| When is a single Gameweek enough? | Elite form + very weak opponent + flawed DGW alternatives. |
| Who gets the armband? | Secure minutes, penalties, centre of attack, high ceiling. |
| Safe or differential? | Protecting a lead → safe; chasing → differential. |
| Biggest mistake | Playing TC for "two fixtures" without analysing role, minutes and matchups. |
| Always remember | Process > result; check team news and rotation risk before deadline. |
